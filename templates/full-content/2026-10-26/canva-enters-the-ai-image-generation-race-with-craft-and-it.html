AI can generate all kinds of pretty pictures, but getting it to spell a headline correctly or keep a poster’s layout intact can be another story. Canva wants to fix that with Canva Craft, its first in-house image generation model. Rather than focusing primarily on photographs, Craft is designed around the posters, presentations, social media graphics, and other visual content people actually create. The goal is simple: spend less time fixing AI’s mistakes and more time making something useful.

Canva Craft plans before it generates

Craft uses a two-stage process. First, it interprets a prompt and plans the image’s layout, including object placement, scale, visual hierarchy, and text. It then generates the image at up to 2K resolution, supporting 30 aspect ratios for different formats. Canva says this approach helps produce more accurate details and readable text while allowing users to edit specific elements without unintentionally changing the rest of the image.

Introducing Canva Craft 🚀 Extraordinary image generation is now available to everyone, powered by our first frontier model built specifically for design. With Craft, Canva is now the world’s second most advanced AI lab for image generation on global leaderboard.

The model was trained on millions of human-created Canva templates, including menus, posters, infographics, presentations, and typography-heavy designs, rather than relying primarily on photographs. That focus could make Craft particularly useful for marketing graphics and social posts, where getting the words and layout right matters as much as the imagery. Its output can also be converted into layered, editable designs using Canva’s Magic Layers technology.

Craft is coming to more than Canva

Craft is available through Canva’s Dream Lab, with access subject to the platform’s account and usage limits. Developers can also access it through Canva’s API, while integrations with third-party AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Gemini are planned through Canva’s MCP. The company is also developing two faster, more cost-efficient variants called Craftfast and Craft-ultrafast.

Canva is entering a crowded field dominated by image models from OpenAI, Google, and other AI companies. But its focus isn’t simply generating impressive images. If Craft can reliably produce visuals that are ready to use rather than needing an hour of cleanup, Canva could make AI a genuinely useful design assistant instead of another source of creative busywork.
