Offloading bounded decisions cut agent LLM calls by 29.6% on 360 SOP-Bench cases

Disclosure: I built Proceda, the SOP-execution harness used here, and ran these experiments.

I wanted to see how much work an agent could hand to a decision model when the answer is narrower than general-purpose text generation. I tested Qwen 3.8 27B with hosted Jev on 360 SOP-Bench cases across referral abuse detection, email intent, and patient intake.

I tried two insertion points:

  1. Step completion after tool calls. Jev checks whether the necessary tool calls have been made and whether the evidence is sufficient to finish the current step. If both yes probabilities reach 0.8, the harness advances without the follow-up LLM turn. Otherwise Qwen continues. Tools still execute.
  2. Bounded business decisions. For email intent, Jev receives the email, original policy, and allowed outcomes. A valid choice with probability at least 0.8 is accepted; uncertain decisions fall back to Qwen. The rest of the SOP still runs.

With both changes enabled:

  • Main-LLM calls fell from 2,537 to 1,785: 29.6% fewer.
  • Estimated execution API cost, including Jev, fell from $6.41 to $4.70: 26.6% lower.
  • Mean case latency fell from 8.64s to 6.94s: 19.7% lower.
  • Final tasks correct were 360/360, compared with 357/360 for Qwen alone.

There is a tradeoff worth separating out: completion checking alone saved 23.8% of LLM calls but finished 356/360 tasks correctly, versus 357/360 for the baseline. The combined result above is from this particular 360-case subset; it does not establish a general accuracy improvement. Including the final structured-plan preparation cost also reduces the estimated cost saving to 24.6%.

The useful architectural pattern is to supply evidence, define allowed choices, validate the result, and keep an LLM fallback. The implementation and experiment reports are public:
