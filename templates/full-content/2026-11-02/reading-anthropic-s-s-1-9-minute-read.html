Anthropic filed its S-1 on October 26. It is the first public look at the company's finances.

$16.3B first-half 2026 revenue, 14× a year earlier

$974M Q2 2026 operating profit, the first ever

$637B in compute contracts, 39× half a year of revenue

30× in five quarters

A year ago Anthropic was doing under $1B a quarter. Now it's doing $11.6B, and the last quarter alone was more than double the one before.

$41M of revenue in all of 2023, $386M in 2024, $4.6B in 2025. The first six months of 2026 brought in more than three times all of last year.

First profitable quarter

The usual worry about AI labs is that every dollar of revenue costs more than a dollar of compute. Chips and data centers are expensive, and the more people use the product, the more you spend.

That was true here until this year. Below the dashed line, compute cost more than it brought in.

Revenue grew far faster than the compute bill. Each new dollar of revenue cost less to serve than the last, which is called operating leverage. Q2 2026 ended with $974M of operating profit, the company's first profitable quarter.

The compute line also includes training new models, so the margin on serving customers is higher than this chart shows.

Where the money goes

Over the full first half, costs were still slightly ahead of revenue. About 80% of revenue is usage-based: businesses call Claude through the API and pay by the token, like a utility bill. Compute eats more than half of all spending. The thin red band is the $693M gap, which Anthropic paid from cash.

The $80.7B paper loss

Most of the $80.7B net loss is an accounting entry. Almost all of it is one line: a "fair-value remeasurement." Some early investors put money in through convertible notes and SAFEs, which are IOUs that turn into shares later. When Anthropic's valuation shot up, those IOUs became worth a lot more, and accounting rules make the company book that increase as a loss.

No cash moved. Operating cash flow was about $3.9B positive, and Anthropic held $131.7B of cash and investments in June.

Who pays

The number of customers spending $100K+ a year rose more than 12× in twelve months. At the top, about 250 customers each spend $10M or more a year.

Net revenue retention was about 900%. That means the average business customer spent nine times more this year than last.

Almost half comes through Amazon, Google and Microsoft's cloud marketplaces, and all three also build competing models. A fifth comes from just two customers. The S-1 says that share is shrinking.

The $637B compute bill

Anthropic has signed deals with seven companies for chips, data centers and cloud capacity, worth roughly $637B in total. That's 39 times what it made in the first half of the year. Not all of it is locked in. Some amounts are ceilings and some deals can be canceled. But the part Anthropic must pay no matter what was $265.6B as of June, and most of it runs to 2033 or later.

The costs are fixed for years. The revenue is mostly pay-as-you-go, and customers can cut back any month. If demand slows, the contract payments do not.

Bull vs. bear

The optimistic read The worried read
Margins The cost to do the same task keeps falling ~10× a year, faster than prices. Margins keep widening. Open-source models get good enough for routine work and push prices down.
Demand Maybe 1M companies could spend $100K+ a year. Anthropic has ~12K. It has barely started. Turning a $1M pilot into company-wide spend needs proof of ROI that may not show up.
Capital There is enough cash and borrowing room to fund the contracts for years. The whole AI industry is borrowing so much it could push up interest rates for everyone.
The bottleneck The real risk is not building enough, as AI agents use far more compute than chat. The S-1's own risk section is mostly about chips, power and data centers, not customers.

Risks

The risk factors run more than 90 pages, about a quarter of the filing. Anthropic writes that its models "may behave in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways" and mentions "catastrophic or existential" risks that insurers might not cover.

Some are already happening:

  • The government fight. The Department of War labeled Anthropic a "supply chain risk" after it refused some military uses. Anthropic lost a 2–1 court ruling in September and asked for a rehearing.
  • Copyright. It agreed to a $1.5B settlement over books, with $1.2B still unpaid in June. More suits are pending.
  • Control. The founders keep 50.1% of the votes. As a public benefit corporation, Anthropic is allowed to pick the public good over shareholder returns.

What's next

  • Q3 revenue. The S-1 leaves it blank. Reuters reports $16.8B, which is still growth but a big slowdown from the quarter before. Part of that may come from Anthropic cracking down on distillation, where others copy its models. Price cuts could speed things back up.
  • The IPO price. An updated filing will set the range. Expectations run from an $80B to a $120B raise, which would make it the biggest IPO ever.
  • The bill coming due. Most of those compute contracts start in 2026 and 2027. Costs will climb before we know whether revenue keeps pace.
