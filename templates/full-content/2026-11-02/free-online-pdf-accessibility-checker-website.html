What gets checked in your PDF

We check every machine-verifiable success criterion of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), and every machine-verifiable test in PDF/UA (ISO 14289).

Structure

  • Is the reading order correct?
  • Are heading levels correctly nested?
  • Are lists structured correctly?
  • Do tables have associated headers?

Content

  • Are fonts embedded and mapped to text?
  • Do images have alt text?
  • Is a valid document language set?
  • Does it have a title and bookmarks?

Presentation & interaction

  • Does text meet WCAG contrast minimums?
  • Do form fields have labels and tags?
  • Are links tagged and described?
  • Is the tab order set correctly?

Note: These are the most common checks. There are many more we run on top of these.

Inaccessible PDFs fail your users, and put you at legal risk

Easy to overlook, expensive to ignore.

Excluded users

People who rely on screen readers, keyboard navigation, or other assistive technology depend on a PDF being properly tagged. When it isn’t, they can’t read it properly, if at all. Information everyone else can access stays out of reach.

Legal exposure

Major accessibility laws, including the ADA, Section 508, and the EAA, require the documents you publish to be accessible. Inaccessible PDFs put you on the wrong side of those laws and leave you exposed to complaints and legal action.
