In August 2026, we released MAGI-2 Preview (~114B total parameters, ~6B active), a research release introducing our Multi-Head Latent MoE architecture alongside our early findings on scaling video generation efficiently.

Today, we are launching MAGI-2—our full-scale flagship model built by scaling model capacity, systems, and data together, paired with targeted post-training and 1080p upscaling. MAGI-2 scales the base model more than 4× to 480B total parameters and ~28B active parameters and expands pre-training data roughly 3× to 4T tokens. It generates up to 30 seconds of 1080p, 25 fps native audio-video, supports omni-reference across images, video, and audio (up to 20 media items per native request), precision timeline control, and video editing.

Scaling these foundations in lockstep delivered steady drops in validation loss, a step-change in human preference—raising our 4-task average Elo on Artificial Analysis Video Arena v2.1 by +151.8 points over MAGI-2 Preview (998.5 → 1150.3)—and early emergent behaviors we did not train for explicitly. Below, we begin with four representative examples of what this scale unlocks, then walk through our model & systems, 4T-token data pipeline, and post-training & 1080p upscaling, followed by what emerges at 480B × 4T scale. We will follow up soon with open-source releases and a detailed technical report covering full architectural specifications, system details, and evaluations.

2. Built to Scale to 480B: Multi-Head Latent MoE and Systems Co-Design

MAGI-2 Preview validated our single-stream Transformer paired with Multi-Head Latent MoE—processing text, video, and audio tokens in one continuous sequence so motion, lip-sync, and acoustics evolve together. In MAGI-2, we scale this backbone more than 4×, from 114B total (6B active) to 480B total (28B active).

Scaling video generation to hundreds of billions of parameters makes sparsity essential, yet simply enlarging a standard MoE quickly runs into distributed-systems bottlenecks. Our decision to commit to Multi-Head Latent MoE from Preview to 480B is driven less by pure modeling gains than by hardware and systems scalability—designing the architecture from day one so sparse models at 480B scale convert FLOPs into real multi-node throughput.

In standard Expert Parallel, the model routes each token dynamically before dispatching full tokens across devices. This route-then-dispatch pattern causes cross-device communication to scale linearly with top-k while making per-device token counts fluctuate with routing outcomes—an imbalance often amplified on heterogeneous multimodal sequences mixing video, audio, and reference inputs—leading to stragglers, pipeline bubbles, peak memory spikes, and synchronization overhead. Multi-Head Latent MoE addresses this at the architectural level by partitioning each token into independent heads, each with its own router and expert pool. This enables Head Parallel: activation slices are exchanged statically by head ownership first, and data-dependent top-k routing stays completely local. Cross-device communication becomes independent of top-k with statically known tensor shapes, eliminating the communication unpredictability of large-scale sparse training.

To turn this architectural advantage into sustained multi-node throughput at 480B scale, the entire training stack was engineered for scaling: Head Parallel and MagiFSDP decouple static inter-node slice exchange from sharded expert parameters, gradients, and optimizer states; MagiMoE optimizes local execution across thousands of narrow experts per layer while overlapping shared-expert computation, activation recomputation, and weight-gradient computation with communication; and MagiMuon scales the Muon optimizer efficiently to sharded expert matrices. Under matched total FLOPs and each architecture’s optimal configuration for 480B pre-training, Multi-Head Latent MoE achieves roughly a 22% training speedup over LatentMoE.

Controlled ablations confirm that this systems-first design also scales cleanly in modeling quality: under matched total and active parameter budgets, an 8-head Multi-Head Latent MoE achieves consistently lower validation loss than Standard MoE and slightly outperforms LatentMoE (the 1-head special case) over longer runs. 3. Scaling Data to 4T Tokens Without the Filtering Trap

4T tokens backed by high-throughput data infrastructure. Scaling a 480B video backbone demands data volume and ingestion throughput built for the same order of magnitude. For MAGI-2, we expanded pre-training data roughly 3× over Preview to 4T tokens. Reaching this scale required an efficiency-first data pipeline engineered for extreme throughput—annotating 4T tokens across roughly 1 billion samples (averaging ~4k tokens per sample) in three weeks.

Avoiding the data filtering trap as capacity grows. Just as important as volume is what we chose to keep. Continuing our lesson from MAGI-2 Preview, we avoided aggressively filtering out complex footage for uniform aesthetics—preserving multi-character interactions, rapid cuts, on-screen text, and dense real-world soundscapes throughout pre-training. When model capacity and unfiltered real-world data scale together to 480B × 4T tokens, many low-level structural failures that smaller models needed heavy post-training patching to fix (such as extra fingers or motion collapse) largely recede during pre-training itself.

4. Post-Training: RL and 1080p Upscaling

From structural patching to visual craft. With structural coherence and motion stability established by 480B × 4T pre-training, post-training shifts from fixing basic defects to elevating visual craft. Rather than spending reinforcement learning capacity correcting basic anatomy or continuity breaks, RL concentrates directly on visual and acoustic richness—sharpening lighting depth, surface micro-textures, fur, and material realism across long sequences.

Latent spatiotemporal upscaling with reference re-injection. Extending that fidelity to full 1080p at 25 fps, a dedicated latent spatiotemporal upscaler lifts base-model outputs while re-injecting the original reference images into the upscaling stage. Because the base generation already anchors where faces and text move across frames, re-supplying the raw references lets the upscaler draw directly on original appearance cues when reconstructing fine details—substantially improving small-face identity and text legibility across 30-second scenes.

5. Emergent Pre-Training Capabilities and Shifts After Post-Training

While evaluating our 480B pre-trained checkpoint, we unexpectedly noticed behaviors well outside standard visual quality and camera control. To rule out help from an external language model rewriting prompts (Prompt Enhancement, PE)—and verify that these behaviors truly came from the video model itself—we ran follow-up probes with PE turned off. Even without PE, the pre-trained model still showed early emergent signs of visual reasoning, implicit narrative understanding, and cross-lingual instruction following:

Visual Reasoning Puzzles.

Across shape-marking tasks, symmetry completion, mazes, and Sudoku, the model shows emerging problem-solving ability (alongside failure cases typical of early emergence). On 9×9 Sudoku probes, for example, the model preserves all given digits and fills in the remaining cells so that every row, column, and 3×3 subgrid satisfies 1–9.

Implicit Narrative Completion & Autonomous Directing.

Given only a brief description or high-level beat sheet, the pre-trained model often fills in unprompted transitional actions, character reactions, and shot transitions. In the PE-off example below (a boat dialogue between Lu Su and Zhuge Liang), providing only character/scene references and three story beats—without specifying framing, camera angles, or cut points—allows the model to plan shot composition, perspective switches, and close-ups autonomously.

Cross-Lingual Understanding and Translation.

When prompted to have a character speak in another language, the pre-trained model shows early ability to translate and generate target-language dialogue directly—without an external LLM rewriting the script beforehand.

After post-training, however, these behaviors shift noticeably. Fine-tuning on several thousand curated clips delivers a clear jump in visual polish, yet the reasoning, implicit storytelling, and cross-lingual behaviors observed in pre-training become noticeably harder to elicit. Our working hypothesis is that fine-tuning on a small, aesthetics-focused dataset narrows the output distribution toward cinematic polish at the expense of broader pre-trained generalization—similar to the “alignment tax” observed in LLM post-training.

This observation prompts us to rethink how we evaluate and optimize video foundation models: visual polish is no longer the only metric that matters—following rules, organizing causal events, and integrating cross-modal cues are equally important. Preserving general pre-trained capabilities while raising visual quality, and evaluating them independently, will directly inform how we design our next generation of video training data.

6. Limitations and Looking Ahead

MAGI-2 still has clear room to improve: extreme high-speed motion can occasionally produce trajectory or continuity artifacts; medium- and wide-shot 2D animation can appear overly smooth in fine local motion; complex soundscapes still have room for richer layering and physical realism; and preserving pre-trained generalization through post-training remains an active research focus.

Scaling from the 114B Preview to the 480B, 4T-token MAGI-2 further validates scaling Multi-Head Latent MoE, systems, and data together—paired with targeted post-training and 1080p upscaling—as a foundation for frontier video generation. Next, alongside continued advances in model capability, data methodology, and inference efficiency, we will open-source MAGI-2 and publish a comprehensive technical report, and continue pushing video foundation models forward with the community.
