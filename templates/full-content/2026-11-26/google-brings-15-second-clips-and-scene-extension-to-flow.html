Google Flow has been the company’s AI filmmaking workbench since it launched at I/O. Now, the official Flow account said Gemini Omni Flash has “leveled up” in Flow, with longer generations, scene extension, and more precise editing. Google introduced Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash earlier this month, and this post is a clearer look at what that upgrade actually lets you do.

What you can actually do with it

You can now generate a continuous scene of up to 15 seconds, in a 360p draft or at 720p, without stitching shorter clips together. For example, one of Flow’s demos takes a room scene you like and restages it as a gloomy blue-hour rainstorm, while keeping the furniture, faces, and camera move the same. You can extend a clip on the timeline by up to 10 seconds per prompt, and attach image references if you want a new character or prop to appear.

That said, you still have to deal with usage limits. According to the official help docs, users without a Google AI plan can generate and edit clips from 3 to 10 seconds, while Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscribers get a 15-second window, plus extensions and upscaling. Another thing to keep in mind is that the frames-to-video and clip extensions work only on the web.

Who can use it, and what it costs

Flow is available to users who are 18 years old or older in supported countries. Each user receives 50 Flow credits daily. Without a subscription, you may run into restrictions on generating videos during peak hours, which usually occur from 2 p.m. to 5 p.m. UTC. Creating a 10-second clip costs 10 credits at 360p resolution or 30 credits at 720p. So the daily free allowance may not last long if you frequently re-roll your videos. Subscribers receive additional monthly credits and can upscale a 360p draft to 1080p or even 4K with the Ultra plan. More details can be found in the official help doc.

As these models get better and better, it makes sense that Google also adds a SynthID watermark to all outputs. Plus, a visible mark is applied automatically for users in India, South Korea, and Vietnam who aren’t on the Ultra 20x plan.

That said, 15-second clips still aren’t ideal for many use cases, but at least you won’t have to keep generating multiple clips to get a single scene sorted out. With this in place, Google plans to retire older Flow models, including Omni Flash 1.1, on December 5.
