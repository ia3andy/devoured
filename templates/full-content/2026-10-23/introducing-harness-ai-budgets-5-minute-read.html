What's new: AI Budgets

AI Budgets set a real limit on AI spend, scoped to whatever unit your organization actually manages against: team, developer, model, or project. Once a budget is live, every dollar is attributed, every threshold fires on time, and the audit trail fills itself.

AI Budgets runs in the same Cost Management Agent that already attributes cloud, AI, and engineering spend to an owner and a reason. A budget only means something if the agent already knows, down to the session or the model, where the money is going, so budgets are accurate from day one. Every warning, block, and approved increase is logged to the same audit trail Harness uses across FinOps, DevOps, and security governance, and that record is ready to export for SOC 2, ISO 27001, or your next FinOps review.

How it works

  • Set the budget at the level that matters. Scope budgets to the unit your organization actually manages spend against, whether that’s a team, developer, model, or project.
  • A weekly cadence. A monthly budget lets someone burn the whole forecast in week one. A weekly reset keeps the feedback loop tight and makes a breach cheap to recover from.
  • Graduated notification. At 90% of budget, spend moves into a WARN tier: the person is notified, but nothing is restricted yet. That first signal is just a nudge.
  • A real limit past 100%. Once weekly spend crosses the budget, access pauses until the next weekly reset or until someone approves a request for more.
  • Self-service requests, tiered by amount. Raising a budget is a same-day, in-product request rather than a ticket. Small increases clear quickly, and larger ones go to the person with the authority to approve them.

A warning at 90%, a hold at 100%, and a one-click path back to access, no ticket required.

Visibility first, enforcement second

Before any budget goes live, the Cost Management Agent attributes spend by user, provider, model, and token type, and surfaces a single number worth tracking: cost per million tokens. Total spend tells you how much was used. Cost per million tokens tells you how well it was used. A team can double its usage and still cut its bill if it's using the right model for the job.

The agent also surfaces token-level detail, input, output, cached, and reasoning tokens, so cache behavior that's quietly driving hidden cost is visible instead of buried in a monthly total.
