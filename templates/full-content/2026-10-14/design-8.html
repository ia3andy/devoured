Every color, by family

Choosing a color takes a second. Finding out that nothing readable sits on it, or that it turns to mud on press, takes a lot longer. Don’t worry, Rhapsody has done all that work for you.

Common questions

How do I find a color’s hex code?

Type it into the search box above. A name, or a hex you already have. Every hex has a page, named or not, and it carries the RGB, HSL, CMYK, and OKLCH alongside.

Which text color is readable on a background color?

Each color’s page measures it, against both WCAG 2.2 contrast and APCA, and says which of black, white, and the color’s own tonal steps clear the bar rather than leaving you to guess.

Will this color print the way it looks on screen?

Often not, and each page says so specifically: the shift is measured against real press profiles and reported as a color difference, not as a warning that every saturated color would trip.

How many color families are there?

Twenty-seven here, each a sub-family of the ISCC-NBS system, the standard the US National Bureau of Standards published for naming colors, rather than a set someone chose.
