What the heck is going on with OpenAI internal AI R&D time horizons: a thread of musings

https://twitter.com/tobyordoxford/status/2112939795630747691

Here is the time horizon data that OpenAI shared publicly

POINT 1/ There are two ways to extract METR-style "50%-accuracy time horizon" from this data:

https://x.com/_Chris_Ong/status/2096617627854811412

Method 1: look for 50% on the y-axis, draw a straight line right until you hit one of OpenAI's linearly-interpolated curves

  1. 50% time horizon: ~10 hours + 68-day doubling time
  2. 80%: 15 minutes + 585-day doubling time

-> 80% reliability is low and rising very slowly

https://twitter.com/ramez/status/2107839121402290643

Method 2: fit a logistic distribution and read off the fitted time horizon

  1. 50% time horizon: ~6 hours + 63-day doubling time
  2. 80%: 15 minutes + 86-day doubling time

-> 80% reliability is low but rising very quickly!

https://x.com/AlexBarry4/status/2107842198746153300

To emphasize:

(a) if you fit 80%-accuracy time horizon 'naively', then it doesn't look like 80% time horizon is rising so quickly;

(b) if you fit a la METR, then we're doubling every 2 months

I don't think one of these is obviously better, except that the latter matches the METR methodology, which probably makes things more comparable

But otherwise not sure what to make of the difference in results!

Method 3: But actually, fitting a Weibull distribution fits the data yet better (see Gus Hamilton and @tobyordoxford's prior writings on fitting a Weibull distribution). This again shows an 80% time horizon (and 50%) doubling v quickly... tobyord.com/writing/hazard…

POINT 2/ Why are OpenAI AI R&D time horizons growing so quickly!!!

METR time horizon, until measurement ended, was doubling every 4-7 months

OpenAI time horizons are doubling every 2 months...

This is especially confusing since @Kwathomas0 had a very important analysis showing that:

  1. METR measures time horizon for software tasks
  2. if you look at other tasks, the LEVEL of time horizon may differ, but the SPEED of increase is similar: 4-7 month doubling time

Explanation (a): there just was a giant overhang of AI research capabilities that OpenAI researchers weren't making use of. Maybe but meh

Explanation (b): time horizon progress is not uniform across domains - this would be unfortunate (because it would make the world harder to predict) 🥲

Explanation (c): time horizon progress has accelerated across domains - this may be consonant with the latest METR report on acceleration at Anthropic... 😐

https://x.com/nikolaj2030/status/2118776619687133579

POINT 3/ Why is the level of time horizons for OpenAI internal AI R&D so low?

At 80% accuracy, it was only 15-20 minutes as of July. That's not very long!

https://twitter.com/tobyordoxford/status/2104875386999873889

(GDM's internal AI R&D 80% time horizon is also ~15 minutes, interestingly)

https://twitter.com/tobyordoxford/status/2112944732930220392

Possibility (a): this is just a difficult domain. Like the Kwa cross-domain graph we saw earlier: different intercepts by domain

Possibility (b): METR measured time horizon using people with "low context". If you measured METR time horizon for people with higher context, time horizons were ~10x lower. Perhaps this explains why OpenAI time horizons are so low in levels

https://x.com/AlexBarry4/status/2113001661673222453

But also! If you measure METR time horizon with "high context" humans instead of the low-context baseliners, then Kwa et al report that time horizon stops being as useful to predict AI capabilities.

POINT 4/ the ratio of p50/p80 time horizons for OpenAI are a lot higher than for METR!

With METR, the 50% time horizon was ~4x the 80% time horizon

At OpenAI, the 80% time horizon is ~15x the 50% time horizon!

I'm not sure what to make of that. Perhaps again related to low- vs. high-context human baselines. Or maybe these p50/p80 ratios vary by field substantially...

5/ Main conclusions:

  1. OpenAI AI R&D time horizon is lower than METR headline... (80% accuracy = ~15min in July)
  2. ...but rising v quickly (~2 month doubling time), though this is somewhat sensitive to specification
