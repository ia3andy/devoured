109 — What an H100 actually costs per hour

On-demand GPU rental prices across AWS, Azure and the neoclouds, normalised to dollars per GPU per hour and grouped so only identical GPUs are compared.

4.57× between cheapest and dearest, for the same chip. One GPU running continuously for a month is $1,964 or $8,972 depending only on where you rent it.

The comparison is grouped, deliberately

"An H100" is three different products. The SXM 80GB, the PCIe 80GB and the NVL 94GB all get marketed as H100s and they do not cost the same. Putting them in one chart manufactures a spread that isn't there, so every comparison here is within a single (model, memory) group.

The same applies within a vendor: Azure lists the same A100 PCIe 80GB at one, two and four GPUs per VM for $3.67, $4.78 and $4.78 per GPU-hour. The spread uses each vendor's cheapest route to a given GPU, so an Azure-vs-Azure gap never shows up as a market gap. RunPod Secure and Community are counted as one company.

What the 4.57× is not

It is not pure margin. Going from Azure's ND series to RunPod Community gives up:

  • InfiniBand. Azure's ND SKU has 400 Gbps per GPU of Quantum-2 InfiniBand with GPU Direct RDMA, which is what makes multi-node training work. That is the reason the ND series is priced above Azure's own single-H100 NC SKU ($6.98).
  • A datacentre SLA. RunPod Community is third-party hosts renting out their own hardware.
  • Enterprise compliance, committed capacity, and the rest of the hyperscaler surface.

If you are fine-tuning on one node, you are paying for an interconnect you will never use. If you are training across 64 nodes, the cheap row is not an option.

Limitations

  • On-demand list prices only. Reserved, committed-use and spot pricing all cut the hyperscaler numbers, and spot in particular would narrow the gap a lot.
  • Single region each (AWS us-east-1, Azure eastus).
  • GPU counts come from vendor documentation, not from the price label — AWS reports "640 GB HBM3" for a p5 and Azure reports an accelerator-memory total, and neither states a per-GPU model. Doc URLs are in compare.json.
  • Lambda is excluded from the headline spread. Its published page states the listed rates take effect 2026-11-16; current rates are behind a login. Its rows are shown separately and labelled.
  • Availability is not priced. A cheap H100 you cannot get is not a price.
