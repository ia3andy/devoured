This month we’re shipping the biggest user experience change to how Octopus looks and works in years. The new experience has been rolling out to Octopus Cloud throughout November, and ships in Octopus Server 2027.0 for self-hosted customers, landing on December 2, 2026.

This year, we made improving the core Octopus experience a priority alongside building new capabilities. Every feature we’ve added over the years made sense on its own, but years of additional complexity were taking a toll on the day-to-day efficiency of how practitioners use our product. Our menus grew long and lacked structure, search only worked inside the space you were already in, and creating a variable halfway through building a process meant leaving the editor entirely. None of this stopped you getting your work done, but it made Octopus harder to use than it should be.

The main changes are:

  • A refreshed visual design that prioritizes your work, objects and statuses above our brand.
  • Global search across your whole Octopus instance via ⌘+K on Mac or Ctrl+K on Windows, with more ways to narrow your search.
  • Clearer navigation grouped around common tasks/outcomes, with collapsible sections that remember your preferences.
  • Refreshed views for Projects, Tasks, Deployment tasks, Insights, Infrastructure Overview and more.
  • Modern, streamlined forms, aiming to reduce overwhelm and optimizing for faster, more intuitive configuration.
  • Better documentation with a focus on improving API/CLI guidance.
  • In-flight variable creation, so you can build deployment processes without breaking flow.

A fresh visual design

The first thing you’ll notice is how Octopus looks. We’ve reworked our color palette and moved our typography down from bold, heavy weights to lighter semi-bold and medium weights. Pages are calmer, easier to scan and more focused on your task at hand.

Your work comes first

You might notice the Octopus logo and many of the colorful buttons and links are now monochrome inside the product. This one sparked some lively debate inside our team. Some people love it, and others miss that little touch of blue.

We’ve kept it mono because we believe our brand should take less attention than your work. In Octopus, the only color asking for your attention should be color you added yourself via Project and Space icons, or the status of your environments and deployments. Our chrome gets out of the way.

Global search

Practitioners spend their days in IDEs, terminals and dev tools where you can jump to anything with a few keystrokes. We heard clear feedback from customers that Search in Octopus wasn’t keeping up with their expectations.

Our previous search was scoped to only search within a Space. To find and navigate to a Project, you first had to know which space it lived in, and navigate to that space before performing the search.

Find more, from anywhere

Global search now works across your whole Octopus instance. Find deployment targets, tenants, accounts, certificates and more across all your spaces and projects, then jump straight there.

Keyboard-first

Open search with ⌘+K (Mac) or Ctrl+K (Windows) and find what you’re looking for without leaving the keyboard. We’ve based this on the command palette patterns you already know from tools like GitHub, Slack, Linear and VS Code. You spend more time in those tools than in Octopus, so we’ve chosen to follow conventions you already know rather than make you learn new ones.

Narrow your search more flexibly

Searching a large instance can return a lot of results, so there are two quick ways to narrow them down.

  • type: Scope your search to a specific type. For example, type:Project.
  • in: Use in to scope your search to only results within the object you choose. For example, in:Payments api-key.

Take action from more places

Create feeds, add certificates, create deployment freezes and more, without the toil of navigating to these actions manually.

Clearer navigation

Navigation is where you’ll notice a few things have moved, so we think it’s particularly important to explain what’s changed and why.

Octopus has a few levels of scope: things that belong to a project, a space, or apply across your whole instance. Our previous navigation blurred the line between these scopes and made it difficult to understand what scope of impact a configuration change might have.

We also found that our navigation reflected everything Octopus can do rather than what you actually need most of the time. Environments, which you might use every day, had the same visual weight as Certificates, which you set up once and rarely touch again. The Manage section had become a bit of a junk drawer of eleven loosely related items in alphabetical order. While we were proud of all the value and functionality we’ve added to Octopus over time, we weren’t proud of how we’d handled the extra complexity in your day-to-day navigation experience.

  • Clearer scoping: The main side panel brings together your spaces, Platform Hub and configuration, so it’s clear what lives outside the space scope and impacts your whole instance.
  • Core features up front: Projects, Tasks, Insights and Tenants now sit at the top of your space navigation, right where you’d expect them.
  • Grouped by task: Less hunting and mental overload to get to the right page. For instance, in your projects: Releases, Channels, Triggers, Freezes and Settings now live under Deployment Model.
  • Collapsible sections you control: Close the sections you don’t need. They’ll remain closed until you reopen them.
  • Unified page headers to help you jump in and out of a project or a tenant.
  • Project descriptions now sit at the bottom of the project navigation panel and open in their own dialog with full text formatting.

Can’t find something? Try global search. It’s the fastest way to learn where things have moved.

Refreshed views

Some pages in Octopus you visit once a month. Others you keep open all day, checking whether a deployment went out or why something failed. We revisited those everyday views, to see where we could make Octopus feel more refined and streamlined.

  • Projects Overview has a tidier table structure to make it easier to scan.
  • Project Dashboard now includes environments and feature flag status.
  • Environment status gives new insight into what’s recently deployed and what feature flags are active.
  • Tasks now highlights running and failed work via status cards.
  • Infrastructure Overview is now closer to an operational dashboard than an inventory list, so you can spot unhealthy targets before they block a deployment.
  • Deployment task log merges task summary and log into one consolidated view.
  • Insights descriptions are cleaner, and a new report switcher helps you flip between views faster.

Create variables and parameters in-flight

When building or editing a deployment process, you might often realize you need a variable that doesn’t yet exist. Until now, that meant:

Leaving the process editor → Going to the Variables interface → Create the new Variable, and → Return to the Process and update the Step

Now you can create project variables right inside the deployment process editor, including multiple values, scopes and value types. Your new variable is saved to Project Variables straight away and is ready to use in your step.

We’ve done the same for parameters in Platform Hub, so you can add and edit them without leaving the page you’re on.

Modern, streamlined forms

Forms touch roughly a third of the Octopus product surface, so even small inconsistencies create real friction in everyday workflows like shipping a release.

We’ve replaced the old experience with a cleaner form system built around clearer grouping, better spacing, and more consistent components. In particular, you’ll notice:

  • Pages feel less cluttered and more focused.
  • More intuitive and efficient interactions with multi-selects.
  • Better in-line validation.

Better documentation

We made the docs easier to use when you’re in the middle of building, troubleshooting, or automating with Octopus. The public docs site has a cleaner Octopus look, better search, and built-in feedback, so it’s faster to find the right answer and tell us when something misses the mark.

A dedicated API reference

We also replaced the old, hard-to-navigate Swagger page with a dedicated, searchable REST API reference at octopus.com/docs/api. Instead of digging through 1,800 endpoints on your own instance, you can link straight to the API details you need.

Improved standards

Behind the scenes, we’ve tightened our documentation standards, improved the information architecture, and published contribution guidance. That means clearer concepts, sharper how-to steps, better reference material, and a docs experience that keeps improving with practitioner feedback.

New experiences, built with the same care

Polishing and refining doesn’t mean we’ve stopped building. We’ve been thoughtful about how we add new value, so new features feel like they’ve always belonged in Octopus.

At Shipped 2026, you’ll also see:

  • Pipeline Explorer, which shows how your project’s environments, lifecycles, channels and targets connect, without clicking through 10 different pages.
  • Feature flags with automated progressive rollouts, so you can release changes in phases.
  • Flexible project templates in Platform Hub.

Coming to an Octopus near you

  • Octopus Cloud: The new experience is already rolling out to Cloud customers through November.
  • Self-hosted: The changes ship in Octopus Server 2027.0 landing on December 2, 2026, and you’ll see them when you upgrade.
