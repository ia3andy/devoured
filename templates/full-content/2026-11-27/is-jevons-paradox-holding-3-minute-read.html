Thus far in the AI boom, Jevons paradox has been the defining law of inference: as token prices fall, usage explodes enough to push total spend higher. In recent months, that dynamic has weakened. Token consumption shows no sign of slowing, yet spend growth has slowed sharply.

The picture so far

So far in 2026, token volume is up 44x, while total spend is up 7.6x. The divergence became clearer this fall: usage kept climbing, while spend only ticked up marginally since early October. Appetite for tokens is clearly still strong, but buyers are simply getting far more output without increasing their budgets.

Elasticity by quarter

Elasticity measures how strongly usage responds to price, better stated as the percent increase in tokens for every 1% drop in price per token. Above 1, total spend rises as prices fall, which is Jevons paradox. At 1, spend stays flat: buyers take the savings as more tokens. Quarterly elasticity, comparing the four weeks before each quarter’s start against the four weeks before its end (as the ratio of log changes in tokens and price per token), shows the shift clearly. Elasticity held at 5.4 in Q1 and 5.5 in Q2, classic Jevons territory where cheaper tokens drove heavy net-new spend. It dropped to 1.6 in Q3 and sits at 1.2 so far in Q4.

This isn’t the first dip. Elasticity also fell to about 1 in December 2024 and November 2025, and both times it recovered with the next wave of model launches. The current slowdown may be a lull between waves rather than a lasting break.

Month-over-month growth: tokens vs. spend

Plotting month-over-month growth alongside major model launches shows where the two diverged. Through the first half of 2026, spend grew nearly as fast as tokens. Since July, the two have split, with tokens still growing roughly 39% a month while spend growth has failed to keep pace. Importantly, cost per token (especially within closed-model families like Claude or GPT) has continued to decline sharply over the same time period.

What it means

Cheaper inference still fuels massive adoption, as 44x token growth proves, but in recent months it hasn’t brought matching growth in spend. The pattern also differs by customer: enterprises look more cost conscious than AI-native scale-ups, taking more of the price drops as savings rather than as new spend.

It’s important to note that the introduction of truly agentic coding in the beginning of 2026 likely helped Jevons hold as strongly as it did, which raises the question: what will the next leap forward be, should spend growth return to its prior pace?

We’ll keep tracking this as the next launches land.
