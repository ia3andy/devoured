Robots gliding along rails in a hog barn run by Chinese livestock producer Wens Foodstuff Group log the body temperature, feed intake and activity of the pigs in each pen, a Securities Daily reporter saw on a farm visit. Lasaite Robot (Shenzhen), a maker of rail-riding barn robots, counts Wens as an investor and core customer, according to Jiemian.

A list of 55 model uses for robots in farming, published on Nov. 19 by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, names Lasaite as the lead company for rail-mounted robots that check pig health, with Wens as its partner. The ministries had shortlisted the uses in October.

Wens, based in Yunfu in southern China's Guangdong province, sold 29.8 million hogs raised for meat, counting live hogs and fresh pork, from January through October, according to its monthly sales report. Cameras, rail robots and automated feeders now take over or assist staff with rounds, watching animal behavior and adjusting feed at leading farm companies, Wens' chief scientist, Wu Zhenfang, told Shanghai Securities News in September. He said they can already give early warning of disease from pigs' behavior and coughs.

Lasaite's Overhead Rail Vision Robot carries color and depth cameras and temperature and humidity sensors, its product page says. Lasaite says the robot scores body condition, warns of health problems, counts stock and estimates herd weight. It has research centers in Shenzhen and Hefei, in eastern China's Anhui province, and a factory in Hefei.

Wens is one of several leading Chinese livestock companies with that tie to Lasaite, Jiemian reported in July. Lasaite has built more than 10,000 rail robots across all its customers, according to a Nov. 24 post on NetEase's self-publishing platform. The reports don't say how many work in Wens' barns, or how big Wens' stake is.
