The problem

64% of product pages tested had at least one critical accessibility issue.

This year, 39,976 Shopify and 19,424 WooCommerce product pages from stores ranked among the world’s top one million websites were scanned. On 37,874 of them, at least one issue in the highest potential-impact category was discovered.

On a product page, that could mean a customer cannot read important information, use a form or select a buying option.

Critical findings are only part of the picture though. 96% of product pages failed at least one automated accessibility check.

These are not just technical faults. They can stop customers using a product page. In the UK alone, an estimated £17 billion in online spending does not happen each year because people find websites difficult or impossible to use. A customer may use a screen reader, enlarge the page or navigate with a keyboard. And when your product page breaks, they generally don't complain or email you. They just leave and buy from somewhere they can actually use (your accessible competitor, perhaps?).

Understanding the headline

What a critical finding can mean for your customers

Critical is the highest potential-impact rating.

For your customer, a critical finding could make important product information, a form field or option impossible to use. The report identifies the barriers with the highest potential impact and shows how common they are across product pages.

64% of the product pages tested contained at least one critical finding.

Shopify and WooCommerce have both invested significantly in accessibility, particularly in checkout and the parts of the buying journey they directly control.

However, your product page is built from platform code, a theme, apps and plugins, agency or developer work and the store's own content.

Any of those layers can introduce a barrier.

That's why accessibility needs an owner inside the business. Someone needs to find where a problem sits, assign it to the right team or supplier and make sure it gets fixed.

By popularity

More traffic did not mean better accessibility

Product pages from the most popular stores were (marginally) worse-performing.

Among stores ranked between 500,000 and one million, 63% of product pages had a critical accessibility failure. Among stores in the top 100,000, that rose to 69%.

A high-traffic store puts every unresolved accessibility barrier in front of a larger audience. Accessibility needs the same ongoing attention as conversion, performance and security.

What fails most

The accessibility problems found most often

Poor colour contrast was the standout problem. It appeared on 80% of the product pages tested.

Overlays

Accessibility overlays fix... nothing.

Accessibility overlays are often sold as a quick way to make a website accessible. The appeal is obvious: one product promises to take a complicated problem off your hands. However, 62% of the 1,691 product pages where an accessibility overlay widget was detected still had at least one critical accessibility failure.

Installing an overlay is not proof that the accessibility work is done.

What to do next

Start with these six checks on your own product pages

  1. Make text easier to read

    Increase the contrast between text and its background.

  2. Give every link a useful name

    Use link text that explains where the link goes or what it does.

  3. Keep headings in a logical order

    Build a clear page structure without skipping heading levels.

  4. Make tap targets easier to select

    Give buttons and links enough space to be selected accurately on a touchscreen.

  5. Use the same name on screen and behind the scenes

    Use the same wording visually and in the name exposed to assistive technology.

  6. Add useful alternative text to images

    Describe an image’s relevant content or purpose when it cannot be seen.

Bonus: AI and shopping

The same page structure that helps your customers shop also helps search engines and machines understand your products too. Clear headings, useful image descriptions and properly built, labelled form fields help your customers find product information, navigate by keyboard or shop with a screen reader. Those same elements also tell search crawlers and AI assistants what the product is, which options are available and what each button, link and field does.

Method

The research covers 59,400 product pages, all tested using one consistent process. One product page was selected per Shopify or WooCommerce store in HTTP Archive's global top-million website dataset. Every page included in the report received the same mobile Lighthouse accessibility audit through Google PageSpeed Insights.
