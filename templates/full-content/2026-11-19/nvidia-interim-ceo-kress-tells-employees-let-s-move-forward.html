Key Points

  • Nvidia executives addressed employees Wednesday at the first all-hands meeting since Jensen Huang resigned.
  • “Let’s move forward,” Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress told the room, according to a person in the room who spoke to CNBC on the condition of anonymity.
  • Kress also mentioned pay raises for employees, according to the person.

Interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress told employees "let's move forward" on Wednesday during the company's first all-hands meeting since founder Jensen Huang resigned, a person who attended the meeting told CNBC. The person asked not to be named because the meeting was private.

Kress was joined by newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones and hyperscale and high-performance computing head Ian Buck.

Several other senior members of Nvidia's executive team spoke or joined Kress on stage, including longtime company veteran Jeff Fisher, senior vice president of software engineering Dwight Diercks, senior vice president of GPU engineering Jonah Alben, executive vice president in charge of operations Debora Shoquist and co-founder Chris Malachowsky, according to people familiar with the matter.

Kress opened with the message about moving forward and also mentioned pay raises and explained that the yearly review process, when employees typically get pay raises and are granted more stock, would be moved up from March to December. Nvidia shares are down 37% from their 52-week high.

Jones discussed the search for the next CEO, the person who attended the meeting said. The meeting lasted about an hour and was in person as well as streamed on Microsoft Teams.

The general sentiment swirling in the room, according to the person, was that employees want Huang to come back. Employees have spoken up on X in recent days to call for the return of Huang as CEO, and one senior employee reportedly sent a letter to the board warning Nvidia would lose "irreplaceable talent" if it doesn't consider reinstating Huang.

But another person familiar with the matter disagreed, saying that employees welcomed the remarks from leadership as well as the visibility into company leadership. The person asked not to be named because the matter is private.

"The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," Jones said during the company's earnings call on Tuesday.

Some executives shared that they do not agree with Jones. "Why make such a strong definitive statement when the investigation is still playing out," an employee told CNBC. The employee asked not to be named given the sensitivity of the subject.

The meeting was held one day after Huang said he was ready to return as CEO should the board want him. Multiple employees told CNBC that Huang's letter "fired employees up."

Despite recent turmoil, Wednesday's meeting was not out of the ordinary. Nvidia typically holds all-hands the morning after quarterly earnings reports, according to a former employee, who asked not to be named because the matter is private. Kress recapped Tuesday's earnings report, calling it a "great quarter," according to the person who said employees welcomed the remarks and visibility from leadership.

In past meetings, Huang took time to answer questions submitted by employees, according to the former employee. A person familiar with the matter told CNBC that the executives also answered pre-submitted questions in Wednesday's all-hands.

Nvidia shares were down Wednesday, a day after the company reported third-quarter earnings that topped Wall Street expectations and gave no signal that the company was going to slow its growth.
