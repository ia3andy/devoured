Computer Scientists Facilitating Independent Review of a High-profile Open AI and Anthropic Result

Both companies announced their models refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis formulated by UC San Diego computer scientists

In case you missed the global media coverage including The New York Times, UC San Diego computer scientists Russell Impagliazzo and Mohan Paturi as well as MIT computer scientist Ryan Williams are facilitating an independent review of a high-profile result generated by two frontier AI labs, Open AI and Anthropic.

Both companies recently announced that their AI models have refuted the highly influential Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH). If the findings hold, the refutation of ETH (and/or the rise of possible alternatives to ETH) could have broad implications for optimization algorithms and a wide range of applications in chip design, encryption systems used on the Internet, and much more.

Now that the future of ETH is a topic of hot discussion, Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams have stepped into the role of helping the computer science community understand and assess the ETH claim independently.

Impagliazzo and Paturi are both professors in the UC San Diego Department of Computer Science and Engineering. Paturi is also the associate dean for artificial intelligence strategy and implementation at the UC San Diego Jacobs School of Engineering.

The team launched ETH: Community Examination and Discussion, a new blog that serves as a place for researchers to share findings and ideas regarding the future of ETH.

The team plans to hold one or more academic conferences, where verification work will be discussed, along with farther-reaching implications. This week on the blog, the team posted the OpenAI prompt that led to the ETH refutation.

ETH was proposed in 1998 by Impagliazzo and Paturi, with then-computer science PhD student Francis Zane, who is now an independent researcher.
