I spent a lot of time thinking about differential history. What it means to change the world isn’t to have a pile of stuff that people say is yours or a pile of accomplishments that people say are yours. That’s just credit assignment, which is notoriously hard even without all the adversarial noise. No, in order to measure changing the world, you have to think in counterfactuals. If you didn’t exist, how would the course of history be different?

You can take a hardline deterministic position, saying that more or less, there was only one way history was going to go. In which case, history class really was just memorizing a bunch of names, places, and dates, and you structurally cannot change the world. I’ve heard that the difference between belief in Great Man and Causes and Forces is just your time horizon.

So it’s probably already determined how AI goes; determined by some 4th order coefficients in a chaotic function, how the balance stacks up between offense and defense and the forces of centralization vs decentralization. These are facts about the map, the great technological optimization landscape we bring up so many times on this blog.

That said, we’re here.

I watched this OpenAI ad this morning, and it shows me that I don’t think these people understand what will happen either. It shows the form of an office, but with all the real work being done in a datacenter thousands of miles away. Why will the office continue to exist?

And with respect to Anthropic, things don’t usually end up well for the main character. While it’s much easier to imagine a singleton than the ritualistic continuation of offices, that’s likely because we’re primed by sci-fi. Nature did not collapse to a singleton, and most AI looking things require exponentially more resources to see linear returns. If you’ve been playing with agent swarms you see this, is it worth 10x the spend to get the work done twice as fast? Is it worth 100x the spend to get the work done three times faster? It breaks down at some point.

It’s a lot easier to imagine bad outcomes than good ones, but the real thing to watch is how the balance between the attacker and defender has shifted. In computer security and cryptography I still can’t tell how this is going to play out. I’m not even sure which way would benefit the individual vs the state.

Perhaps there are some universal answers to this?

What I know for sure is that the best we can hope for is fair markets (against monopolies, against internal models, against vertical integration). Linear spend for linear amounts of compute. Produce more than you consume and you continue to exist, but that bar isn’t that high.

A good world is where there’s millions of independent power centers, all capable of collaborating with they see fit, and imposing small costs on each other when they don’t. And I can’t tell you what that good world looks like, because in that world there is no central planner. There is no one who says what it looks like, it’s emergent from the collective interactions of millions.

Isn’t that the real American Dream?
