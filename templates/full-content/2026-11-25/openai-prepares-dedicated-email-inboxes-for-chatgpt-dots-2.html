OpenAI is working on email functionality for Dots, its always-on agents inside ChatGPT, along with an option for users to ask a Dot to claim its own email address. The feature is still hidden in the interface and is not available to users yet.

A Dot's profile panel now shows an Email button next to Call and Slack. Selecting it opens a "dot's email" settings dialog, which for now reports that email isn't available for the account and includes a Turn off email option. This setup suggests that each Dot could get a dedicated address that its owner can switch off at any time. For now, a Dot asked about it still replies that it has no email address of its own, can only draft or send messages from a connected Gmail account, and cannot claim an address as its own account.

Dots can already connect to a user's personal email and Slack, and people can text their Dot from a phone, but OpenAI says a Dot cannot have a standalone email address at launch.

Samir, who works on Dots and ChatGPT at OpenAI, has said that Dots will soon get their own addresses, so users could CC a Dot to find a time that works for everyone or forward it a receipt and ask it to negotiate a refund, and that OpenAI has started testing it. A dedicated inbox would let a Dot correspond with others under its own identity, without acting from its owner's personal account. That would matter most for people who already hand off errands and recurring work to their Dot.

OpenAI has not shared a release date beyond saying the feature is coming soon. Once live, the option would most likely sit in the Dot's profile in ChatGPT, where calls and Slack already appear. The move fits OpenAI's push to turn Dots into agents with their own computer, browser, and possibly inbox, instead of assistants that only borrow their owner's accounts.
