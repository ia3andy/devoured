A while back, after seeing how my friend’s company worked, I convinced them to switch to a centralized booking system. Of course, the new system would be built by me.

I sinned while building it. I signed up for a Facebook account. Then a TikTok account. Just to set up the API stuff. I’m not familiar with Facebook’s API, so after trying to walk them through generating API tokens in the Facebook backend via screen sharing, I admitted defeat. I’ll create an account. Just add me. I’ll handle the rest.

I set up a Stripe account for them. Turns out most of their customers don’t pay by credit card. I set up the WhatsApp Cloud API for them. And they were like, what do you mean we can’t just message random people. They preferred their grey-area WhatsApp Web bot.

When you build a system like this, the first thing you do is learn the business logic. You ask a lot of questions, try your best to understand how they work, and implement it. The second thing you do is strip all that business logic away. Whether a customer gets a refund depends on someone’s mood that day, not on system code. Excel has no logic, which is why they love Excel.

One thing I built was their membership passes. The passes had been around for a while, but nobody knew how they worked. Or more like, everybody knew how they worked, but everybody’s understanding was a little different. Passes are for weekdays only. No, we can let a member use them on weekends too. You need 1 credit for package A and 2 for package B. No, it’s 1 credit per customer, and the package doesn’t matter. Also there’s now an old membership and a new membership, each with its own rules.

I thought of the Steve Jobs calculator story, so I built a membership rule maker. Whatever they want, they just type it, and an LLM interprets the message and makes the changes. The disagreements disappeared the moment I told them about the rule maker. They never ended up using it.

Lao Tzu said, 5 colors make you blind, 5 sounds make you deaf. I learned not to add any explanatory text in the UI, because they ignore the text. If a settings page has more than 5 options, they can’t find the one they’re looking for. If you hide something behind a disclosure triangle, they won’t find it. So I refactored the UI and turned everything into a wizard. Do one thing at a time. Use graphics instead of text. 1 screen gets 1 button, maybe 2. It helped a bit.

The system sends notifications to staff. Like when a customer arrives. It wasn’t very effective. Even with the notification, customers still had to wait quite a while. Then one day someone posted a screenshot in the group chat, and I noticed the app’s notification badge. 99+. Okay, now I knew why it was ineffective. Turning the notification frequency down doesn’t help, because every other app on their phones is sending notifications too, and they don’t turn those off. Yours will be the 1000th notification of the day.

Every time I asked a long question in the group chat, they’d go quiet, then come back with an even longer answer. With perfect grammar, nicely formatted with emojis and section headers. I thought, since you’re all AI users, maybe I should add an MCP server to the system. They loved it. Creating a new booking is so much faster now! (The booking form is 1 dropdown and 2 steppers.) Wow, my AI just found the 3 bookings with deposits to return! (It’s right there on the Todo page.)

After watching them rave about the MCP in the group chat, I sat back and thought. Oh my god, what have I done.
